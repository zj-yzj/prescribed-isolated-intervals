\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit construction of a finite set of integers whose $h$-fold
sumset contains any prescribed finite target set and whose remaining elements
are isolated. As a consequence, every sufficiently separated family of
intervals can be prescribed as exactly the nontrivial intervals in an
$h$-fold sumset. This answers the public multi-interval questions numbered
Problems 14 and 15 in version 3 of Nathanson's
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26425}{\emph{Problems in additive number
theory, VII}}. In fact the required gap threshold is the minimal value
$N_q=n+2$ allowed by the formulation, and the construction has
$|A|=2q(n+1)$.

The proof uses a separated-label principle that reduces the construction to
excluding short integer relations among labels. This framework also gives
quantitative diameter refinements: finite moment labels give polynomial
diameter for fixed $h$, Bose--Chowla labels improve the fixed-$h$ exponent,
and shifted-Sidon labels give a quadratic double-sumset bound with a matching
Sidon-method lower bound.

Finally, we study cardinality separately from complete isolation. A two-colour
bounded Frobenius packet gives a single-interval double-sumset construction
with $O(\sqrt n)$ elements, which is optimal in order. Under an additional
pair-sum separation hypothesis on the starts, the same packet gives an
$O(q\sqrt n)$ starts-only multi-interval construction. A second sparse
multi-interval construction uses Chinese-remainder incompatible colours to
remove the pair-sum separation hypothesis, at the cost of a $q$-dependent
large-$n$ threshold and larger spacing between the prescribed intervals. The
main separation, interval-family, cardinality, translation, diameter, and
shifted-Sidon arguments have been checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Nathanson's problem list on additive $h$-bases for intervals asks how much
control is possible over the intervals contained in finite sumsets. In
version 3, posted on June 8, 2026, the single-interval double-sumset case is
constructed directly, and the public multi-interval questions, Problems 14
and 15, ask whether one can
prescribe a separated set
\[
 C=\{c_1<\cdots<c_q\}
\]
of starting points so that the only intervals of length $n$ start at the
elements of $C$, with a stronger version forbidding all additional proper
intervals. The stated problems allow arbitrary fixed $h\geq 2$.

The point of this note is that the multi-interval problem is more naturally a
finite-target problem. Let
\[
 S=\bigcup_{i=1}^q[c_i,c_i+n].
\]
It is enough to construct $A$ so that
\[
 hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
\]
where every point of $E$ is separated from $S$ and from every other point of
$E$ by at least two. Then the maximal nontrivial intervals in $hA$ are
exactly the maximal nontrivial intervals already present in $S$. The
construction below proves this for every $h\geq 2$ and, more generally, for
an arbitrary finite target set $S\subseteq\Z$.

The mechanism is a tagged cancellation construction. To each target point
$t_r$ we attach a large label $a_r$ and put two generators into $A$,
\[
 t_r+(h-1)a_r
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 -a_r.
\]
The intended representation of $t_r$ is the matched sum consisting of one
positive generator and $h-1$ copies of the corresponding negative generator.
All other $h$-term sums carry a nonzero label. If the label family has no
short relations, then these exceptional sums are far apart.

The first version uses powers and has exponential diameter. The later
sections replace the powers by finite moment labels and then by a
Bose--Chowla modular $B_t$ construction, giving polynomial diameter for fixed
$h$ with a smaller exponent. When $h=2$, shifted-Sidon labels give a direct
quadratic diameter bound. The Sidon section also includes a lower bound
showing that this quadratic order is forced by the Sidon-label strategy.
These quantitative refinements are not needed merely to answer the existence
questions, but they separate the flexible cancellation mechanism from the
arithmetic problem of choosing efficient labels.

There is another, complementary optimization in the double-sumset case. If
one asks only for the starts of intervals of length $n$, rather than complete
isolation of every other sum, then an interval need not be generated point by
point. A two-colour packet built from arithmetic progressions with steps $a$
and $a+1$ produces one long interval in the cross-sum while the two same-colour
sumsets have no adjacent elements. This gives an order-optimal sparse
single-interval construction with $O(\sqrt n)$ elements, and an
$O(q\sqrt n)$ multi-interval version whenever the pair sums $c_i+c_j$ are
also separated. A further CRT variant gives the same $O(q\sqrt n)$
cardinality order for arbitrary prescribed starts, provided $q$ is fixed,
$n$ is sufficiently large in terms of $q$, and the prescribed intervals are
separated on the scale of $3n$.

\section{A short separation lemma}

For an integer vector $v=(v_0,\ldots,v_{s-1})$, write
$\lVert v\rVert_1=\sum_r |v_r|$.

\begin{lemma}[Positional separation]\label{lem:separation}
Let $B,M\geq 1$ and $a_r=MB^r$ for $0\leq r<s$. If
$\lambda,\mu\in\Z^s$, $\lambda\neq\mu$, and
$\lVert\lambda\rVert_1+\lVert\mu\rVert_1\leq B$, then
\[
 \left|\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}(\lambda_r-\mu_r)a_r\right|\geq M.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $z=\lambda-\mu$ and let $R$ be the largest index with $z_R\neq 0$.
We have $\lVert z\rVert_1\leq B$. If $R=0$, the result is immediate. If
$R\geq 1$, then
\begin{align*}
\left|\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}z_ra_r\right|
&\geq M\left(|z_R|B^R-\sum_{r<R}|z_r|B^r\right)\\
&\geq M\left(B^R-(B-1)B^{R-1}\right)
\geq M.
\end{align*}
Here the sum of the absolute values of the lower coefficients is at most
$B-|z_R|\leq B-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Separated-label principle]\label{prop:labels}
Let $h\geq 2$, $d\geq 1$, and let
$S=\{t_0,\ldots,t_{m-1}\}\subseteq\Z$ be nonempty. Put
\[
 H=h(h-1),
 \qquad
 T=\max_{t\in S}|t|.
\]
Suppose that $M\geq 2hT+d$ and that integers $a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1}$ satisfy
\[
 \left|\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}(\lambda_r-\mu_r)a_r\right|\geq M
 \tag{1}\label{eq:abstract-separation}
\]
whenever $\lambda,\mu\in\Z^m$ are distinct and
\[
 \lVert\lambda\rVert_1+\lVert\mu\rVert_1\leq 2H.
\]
Then
\[
 A=\{t_r+(h-1)a_r:0\leq r<m\}\cup\{-a_r:0\leq r<m\}
\]
has $|A|=2m$ and
\[
 hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
\]
where distinct elements of $E$ are at distance at least $d$ from one another
and every element of $E$ is at distance at least $d$ from $S$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Attach to the generator $t_r+(h-1)a_r$ the label vector $(h-1)e_r$ and
offset $t_r$. Attach to the generator $-a_r$ the label vector $-e_r$ and
offset $0$. The integer value of a tagged generator is its offset plus the
dot product of its label vector with $(a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1})$.

We first check that the displayed union defining $A$ is disjoint and has the
claimed cardinality. Distinct tagged generators have distinct label vectors.
The sum of the $\ell^1$ norms of two generator labels is at most
$2(h-1)\leq 2H$, and their offsets differ by at most $2T$. Since
$M\geq 2hT+d>2T$, the separation hypothesis
\eqref{eq:abstract-separation} implies that two distinct tagged generators
cannot have the same integer value. Hence $|A|=2m$.

Now consider an arbitrary $h$-term sum from $A$. Let $p_r$ be the number of
times the positive generator $t_r+(h-1)a_r$ is used, and let $u_r$ be the
number of times the negative generator $-a_r$ is used. Thus
\[
 \sum_r(p_r+u_r)=h.
\]
The label coefficient of this representation is
\[
 v_r=(h-1)p_r-u_r.
\]
Its norm is bounded by
\[
 \sum_r |v_r|
 \leq (h-1)\sum_rp_r+\sum_ru_r
 \leq h(h-1)=H,
\]
and the offset $\sum_rp_rt_r$ has absolute value at most $hT$.

The target points are exactly the sums with zero label. Indeed, if $v=0$,
then $(h-1)p_r=u_r$ for every $r$. Therefore
\[
 \sum_r(p_r+u_r)=h\sum_rp_r=h,
\]
so $\sum_rp_r=1$. There is exactly one index $r$ with $p_r=1$, and then
$u_r=h-1$ while all other counts vanish. The corresponding sum is
\[
 (t_r+(h-1)a_r)+(h-1)(-a_r)=t_r.
\]
Conversely, this matched representation gives every $t_r$. Thus the
zero-label part of $hA$ is precisely $S$.

It remains to see that exceptional sums cannot cluster. A nonzero label
vector determines its multiset of terms. Suppose two $h$-term multisets with
counts $(p_r,u_r)$ and $(p'_r,u'_r)$ have the same label vector. Then
\[
 u_r-u'_r=(h-1)(p_r-p'_r).
\]
Summing this identity over $r$ and using the fact that both multisets have
$h$ terms gives $\sum_rp_r=\sum_rp'_r$. If the positive count vectors were
not equal, choose an index with $p_r>p'_r$. Then $p_r\geq 1$ and the
displayed identity gives $u_r\geq h-1$. These already use all $h$ terms, so
the first multiset is a matched zero-label representation, contradicting the
assumption that the common label vector is nonzero. Hence $p=p'$ and then
also $u=u'$.

Consequently, two distinct exceptional sums have distinct nonzero label
vectors. Also, every exceptional sum has a label vector distinct from the
zero label of a target point. For two exceptional sums, or for an exceptional
sum and a target point, the two label vectors have combined norm at most
$2H$. Equation~\eqref{eq:abstract-separation} gives label separation at
least $M$, while the two offsets together can change the distance by at most
$2hT$. The remaining distance is at least
\[
 M-2hT\geq d.
\]
This proves the required decomposition and isolation.
\end{proof}

\section{The construction}

\begin{theorem}[Finite target realization]\label{thm:finite-target}
Let $h\geq 2$, $d\geq 1$, and let $S\subseteq\Z$ be a nonempty finite set
with $m=|S|$. There is an explicitly constructible set $A\subseteq\Z$ with
$|A|=2m$ such that
\[
 hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
\]
where distinct elements of $E$ are at distance at least $d$ from one another
and every element of $E$ is at distance at least $d$ from $S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Enumerate $S=\{t_0,\ldots,t_{m-1}\}$ and put
\[
 T=\max_{t\in S}|t|,
 \qquad M=2hT+d,
 \qquad B=2h(h-1),
 \qquad a_r=MB^r.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:separation} verifies
\eqref{eq:abstract-separation} for these labels: if the two coefficient
vectors have combined $\ell^1$ norm at most $B=2h(h-1)$, then their
positional values differ by at least $M$. Proposition~\ref{prop:labels}
therefore gives the required set
\[
 A=\{t_r+(h-1)a_r:0\leq r<m\}\cup\{-a_r:0\leq r<m\}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Prescribed isolated intervals]\label{thm:main}
Let $h\geq 2$, $n\geq 1$, $d\geq 2$, $q\geq 1$, and
\[
 C=\{c_1<\cdots<c_q\}\subseteq\Z
\]
with $c_{i+1}-c_i\geq n+2$ for all $i$. There is an explicitly constructible
set $A\subseteq\Z$ with $|A|=2q(n+1)$ such that
\[
 hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
 \qquad
 S=\bigcup_{i=1}^q[c_i,c_i+n],
\]
where
\[
 |x-y|\geq d
\]
whenever $x,y\in E$ are distinct or $x\in E$ and $y\in S$.
Consequently, the maximal intervals in $hA$ of length at least one are
exactly the intervals $[c_i,c_i+n]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $c_{i+1}-c_i\geq n+2$, the intervals $[c_i,c_i+n]$ are disjoint
and separated from one another by at least one missing integer. Hence
$|S|=q(n+1)$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-target} to this finite target
set $S$. It gives $|A|=2q(n+1)$, the decomposition
$hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E$, and the required distance bounds.
Since $d\geq 2$ and the intervals in $S$ are separated by at least one
missing integer, no exceptional point can extend a target interval or form a
new nontrivial interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Single interval specialization]\label{cor:single}
For all $h\geq 2$, $c\in\Z$, $n\geq 1$, and $d\geq 2$, there is a set
$A\subseteq\Z$ with $|A|=2n+2$ such that $[c,c+n]\subseteq hA$, every
element of $hA\setminus[c,c+n]$ is at distance at least $d$ from every
other such element and from $[c,c+n]$, and the longest interval in $hA$ has
length $n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with $C=\{c\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Nathanson's public multi-interval problems]\label{cor:problems}
In the notation of Problems 14 and 15 in version 3 of Nathanson's
\emph{Problems in additive number theory, VII}, both problems are true for
every $h\geq 2$.
More precisely, one can take the threshold
\[
 N_q=n+2
\]
and
\[
 k_C=2q(n+1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Problem 15 assumes $N_q\geq n+2$, so $N_q=n+2$ is the smallest possible
threshold compatible with that formulation. If $c_{i+1}-c_i\geq n+2$, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with $d=2$ gives
\[
 hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
 \qquad S=\bigcup_{i=1}^q[c_i,c_i+n],
\]
where every point of $E$ is isolated from every other point of $hA$.

The only intervals of length $n$ in $hA$ are the displayed intervals
$[c_i,c_i+n]$, proving Problem 14. Since every element outside $S$ is
isolated and at distance at least two from $S$, every interval of length one
in $hA$ lies inside one of the intervals $[c_i,c_i+n]$, proving the stronger
Problem 15.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Diameter bound]
For
\[
 S=\bigcup_{i=1}^q[c_i,c_i+n],
 \qquad
 T=\max_{s\in S}|s|,
\]
the construction in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)
 \leq h(2hT+d)\bigl(2h(h-1)\bigr)^{q(n+1)-1}+T.
\]
The bound is intentionally simple rather than optimized.
\end{remark}

\section{A sparse construction for one interval}

The construction above proves the stronger no-extra-interval assertion with
$2q(n+1)$ elements. For a single interval in a double sumset, one can do much
better in the interval length if shorter proper intervals elsewhere are
allowed.

\begin{lemma}[A two-colour Frobenius packet]\label{lem:frobenius-packet}
Let $a\geq 2$, put $b=a+1$, and let $R\geq b-1$, $S\geq a-1$. Define
\[
 X=\{0,a,2a,\ldots,Ra\},
 \qquad
 Y=\{0,b,2b,\ldots,Sb\},
\]
and put
\[
 g=(a-1)(b-1)=a(a-1),
 \qquad
 L=aR+bS-2g.
\]
Then $X+Y$ contains the interval
\[
 [g,g+L].
\]
If $L\geq g$, this is the only interval of length $L$ in $X+Y$. Moreover
$X+X$ and $Y+Y$ contain no nontrivial intervals.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The last assertion is immediate: $X+X$ is contained in one residue class
modulo $a$, and $Y+Y$ is contained in one residue class modulo $b$, with
$a,b\geq 2$.

We use the standard bounded form of the two-generator Frobenius argument: if
$\gcd(a,b)=1$, $R\geq b-1$, and $S\geq a-1$, then
\[
 \{ia+jb:0\leq i\leq R,\ 0\leq j\leq S\}
\]
contains every integer in the interval
\[
 [(a-1)(b-1),\,aR+bS-(a-1)(b-1)].
\]
For completeness, let $N$ be in this interval. Choose
$j_0\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}$ with $N-j_0b\equiv0\pmod a$. Since
$N\geq(a-1)(b-1)$, the integer
\[
 i_0=\frac{N-j_0b}{a}
\]
is nonnegative. If $i_0\leq R$, then $N=i_0a+j_0b$ is already in the box.
Otherwise choose the least integer $t\geq1$ such that
$i_0-tb\leq R$, and put
\[
 i=i_0-tb,\qquad j=j_0+ta.
\]
Then $i\leq R$, and the minimality of $t$ gives $i>R-b$. Since
$R\geq b-1$, this implies $0\leq i\leq R$. If $j>S$, then
$i\geq R-b+1$, and therefore
\[
 N=ia+jb\geq a(R-b+1)+b(S+1)
 =aR+bS-(a-1)(b-1)+1,
\]
contradicting the upper bound. Thus $j\leq S$, and $N=ia+jb$ lies in the
bounded sumset. Applying this with $b=a+1$ gives the interval $[g,g+L]$.

The integer $g-1$ is the Frobenius number for $a$ and $b$, so it is not in
$X+Y$; by symmetry, $aR+bS-g+1$ is not in $X+Y$ either. All points of $X+Y$
outside $[g,g+L]$ lie in the two side ranges
\[
 [0,g-1],
 \qquad
 [g+L+1,g+L+g].
\]
Each side range has diameter $g-1<L$ when $L\geq g$. Therefore no side range
contains an interval of length $L$, and no interval of length $L$ can extend
the central interval across either missing endpoint.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sparse single-interval realization]\label{thm:sparse-single}
Let $n\geq 16$, let $c\in\Z$, and put
\[
 a=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor-1,
 \qquad
 g=a(a-1).
\]
Then there is a finite set $A\subseteq\Z$ such that $[c,c+n]$ is the unique
interval of length $n$ in $2A$, and
\[
 |A|\leq 5\sqrt n+1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 n_0=n-(2a-1).
\]
Since $a=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor-1$ and $n\geq 16$, we have $a\geq 3$ and
$n_0\geq a^2-1$. Choose $v\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}$ such that
\[
 v\equiv n_0\pmod a,
\]
and define
\[
 u=\frac{n_0-(a+1)v}{a}.
\]
The inequality $n_0\geq a^2-1$ gives $u\geq 0$. Set
\[
 R=a+u,\qquad S=a-1+v,\qquad b=a+1.
\]
Then $R\geq b-1$, $S\geq a-1$, and
\[
 aR+bS-2a(a-1)
 =2a-1+au+(a+1)v
 =n.
\]
Also $g<a^2\leq n$.

Let $X$ and $Y$ be the two arithmetic progressions from
Lemma~\ref{lem:frobenius-packet}. Choose an integer $P$ so large that the
three families of sums displayed below are pairwise separated by at least one
missing integer; for example any sufficiently large positive $P$ works. Put
\[
 A=(P+X)\cup(c-g-P+Y).
\]
The cross-sums are
\[
 (P+X)+(c-g-P+Y)=c-g+X+Y.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:frobenius-packet}, this cross-sum packet has a unique
interval of length $n$, namely $[c,c+n]$. The same-colour sums
\[
 (P+X)+(P+X)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (c-g-P+Y)+(c-g-P+Y)
\]
contain no nontrivial intervals, since they are translates of $X+X$ and
$Y+Y$. The choice of $P$ keeps these same-colour sums away from the cross-sum
packet. Hence $[c,c+n]$ is the unique interval of length $n$ in $2A$.

Finally,
\[
 |A|\leq (R+1)+(S+1).
\]
Because $v<a$ and $a\leq\sqrt n$, we have $S+1=a+v\leq 2\sqrt n$. Also
$a\geq \sqrt n/2$ for $n\geq 16$, and hence
\[
 R+1=a+u+1\leq a+\frac{n_0}{a}+1\leq 3\sqrt n+1.
\]
Combining these two estimates gives the claimed bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Optimal order for one interval]\label{prop:sparse-lower}
Let $A\subseteq\Z$ be finite, put $k=|A|$, and suppose that $2A$ contains an
interval of length $n$. Then
\[
 k\geq \frac{\sqrt{8n+9}-1}{2}.
\]
Consequently, the bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-single} has the best
possible order of growth in $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sum $a+a'$ is determined by the unordered pair $\{a,a'\}$, with repetition
allowed. Hence
\[
 |2A|\leq \binom{k+1}{2}.
\]
If $2A$ contains an interval of length $n$, then it contains at least $n+1$
distinct integers. Therefore
\[
 n+1\leq \binom{k+1}{2}=\frac{k(k+1)}2,
\]
which gives the displayed lower bound after solving the quadratic inequality
for $k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sparse pair-separated multi-interval realization]
\label{thm:sparse-pair-separated}
Let $n\geq 16$, let $q\geq 1$, and define
\[
 a=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor-1,\qquad b=a+1,\qquad g=a(a-1).
\]
Put
\[
 n_0=n-(2a-1),
\]
choose $v\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}$ with $v\equiv n_0\pmod a$, and set
\[
 u=\frac{n_0-(a+1)v}{a},\qquad R=a+u,\qquad S=a-1+v.
\]
Let
\[
 C=\{c_1<\cdots<c_q\}\subseteq\Z
\]
and suppose that distinct elements of
\[
 C+C=\{c_i+c_j:1\leq i\leq j\leq q\}
\]
are separated by at least
\[
 \Delta=\max(n+2g+2,\ 2Sb+2).
\]
Then there is a finite set $A\subseteq\Z$ such that the intervals
$[x,x+n]\subseteq 2A$ are exactly the intervals $[c_i,c_i+n]$, and
\[
 |A|\leq (2q+3)\sqrt n+1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-single}, the chosen parameters
satisfy
\[
 R\geq b-1,\qquad S\geq a-1,\qquad aR+bS-2g=n,\qquad n\geq g.
\]
Let
\[
 X=\{0,a,2a,\ldots,Ra\},\qquad
 Y=\{0,b,2b,\ldots,Sb\}.
\]
Choose $P$ so large that the families
\[
 2P+X+X,\qquad c_i-g+X+Y,\qquad
 c_i+c_j-2g-2P+Y+Y
\]
are separated from families of the other two displayed types by at least one
missing integer. This is possible because $P$ moves the first type to the
right and the third type to the left, while the cross-sum packets stay fixed.
Put
\[
 A=(P+X)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^q(c_i-g-P+Y).
\]

The cross-sums are
\[
 (P+X)+(c_i-g-P+Y)=c_i-g+X+Y.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:frobenius-packet}, the $i$th cross-sum packet contains the
unique interval of length $n$
\[
 [c_i,c_i+n].
\]
It is contained in $[c_i-g,c_i+n+g]$. Since the pair-sum separation hypothesis
applied to $2c_i$ and $c_i+c_{i+1}$ implies
$c_{i+1}-c_i\geq\Delta\geq n+2g+2$, the cross-sum packets are separated from
one another by at least one missing integer.

The same-colour $X$-sum $2P+X+X$ has no nontrivial interval, since $X+X$ lies
in one residue class modulo $a$. Likewise each translate of $Y+Y$ lies in one
residue class modulo $b$ and hence has no nontrivial interval. All translates
of $Y+Y$ are contained in intervals of the form
\[
 c_i+c_j-2g-2P+[0,2Sb].
\]
The hypothesis that distinct elements of $C+C$ are separated by at least
$2Sb+2$ keeps these $Y+Y$ packets separated from one another by at least one
missing integer. Therefore no interval of length $n$ can arise from the
same-colour sums or by joining two different packets. The only intervals
$[x,x+n]\subseteq 2A$ are the displayed intervals $[c_i,c_i+n]$.

Finally,
\[
 |A|\leq (R+1)+q(S+1)\leq 3\sqrt n+1+2q\sqrt n,
\]
by the estimates already proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-single}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sparse multi-intervals with incompatible colours]
\label{thm:sparse-crt}
Let $q\geq1$, let $p_1,\ldots,p_q$ be distinct primes at least $5$, and put
\[
 W=6\prod_{i=1}^q p_i.
\]
Let $n\geq1$ satisfy
\[
 \lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\geq 4W,
\]
and let
\[
 C=\{c_1<\cdots<c_q\}\subseteq\Z
\]
with
\[
 c_{i+1}-c_i\geq 3n
 \qquad (1\leq i<q).
\]
Then there is a finite set $A\subseteq\Z$ such that the intervals
$[x,x+n]\subseteq 2A$ are exactly the intervals $[c_i,c_i+n]$, and
\[
 |A|\leq q(5\sqrt n+1).
\]
In particular, for each fixed $q$ this gives a starts-only multi-interval
construction with $O_q(\sqrt n)$ elements and without any pair-sum separation
assumption on $C+C$; uniformly, the displayed bound is $O(q\sqrt n)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $N=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$. For each $i$ choose, by the Chinese remainder
theorem, the unique integer $a_i\in[N-W,N-1]$ satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_i&\equiv0 \pmod 2,\\
 a_i&\equiv-1 \pmod 3,\\
 a_i&\equiv-1 \pmod {p_i},\\
 a_i&\equiv0 \pmod {p_j}\qquad (j\neq i).
\end{aligned}
\]
The interval $[N-W,N-1]$ contains one representative of every residue class
modulo $W$. Since $N\geq4W$, we have $a_i\geq3N/4$; and since
$N=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$, this gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n}{2}\leq a_i\leq \sqrt n-1.
\]
Put $b_i=a_i+1$. Then $\gcd(a_i,b_i)=1$, all $a_i$ are even, all $b_i$ are
divisible by $3$, and for $i\neq j$ the two integers $a_i$ and $b_j$ are both
divisible by $p_j$.

For each $i$, repeat the parameter choice from
Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-single} with $a_i$ in place of $a$. Thus
\[
 g_i=a_i(a_i-1),\qquad n_{0,i}=n-(2a_i-1),
\]
choose $v_i\in\{0,\ldots,a_i-1\}$ with $v_i\equiv n_{0,i}\pmod {a_i}$, and
put
\[
 u_i=\frac{n_{0,i}-(a_i+1)v_i}{a_i},\qquad
 R_i=a_i+u_i,\qquad S_i=a_i-1+v_i.
\]
Because $a_i+1\leq\sqrt n$, the same estimates as before give
$u_i\geq0$, $R_i\geq b_i-1$, $S_i\geq a_i-1$, and
\[
 a_iR_i+b_iS_i-2g_i=n,\qquad g_i<n.
\]
Let
\[
 X_i=\{0,a_i,2a_i,\ldots,R_i a_i\},
 \qquad
 Y_i=\{0,b_i,2b_i,\ldots,S_i b_i\}.
\]
Then Lemma~\ref{lem:frobenius-packet} shows that
$c_i-g_i+X_i+Y_i$ has the unique interval of length $n$ equal to
$[c_i,c_i+n]$.

It remains to keep all unmatched colour sums from interacting. Choose
positional labels $\ell_i=M4^{i-1}$ with $M$ so large that packets carrying
different nonzero label vectors are separated from one another and from all
zero-label packets by at least one missing integer. To make this precise,
give every element of $X_i$ the label vector $e_i$ and every element of $Y_i$
the label vector $-e_i$. Thus the two-term packet $X_i+X_j$ has label
$e_i+e_j$, the packet $X_i+Y_j$ has label $e_i-e_j$, and the packet
$Y_i+Y_j$ has label $-e_i-e_j$. The only zero-label packets are the matched
packets $X_i+Y_i$.

Let $T_0$ be the maximum absolute value of an element occurring in any
unlabelled two-term packet
\[
 X_i+X_j,\qquad c_j-g_j+X_i+Y_j,\qquad
 c_i+c_j-g_i-g_j+Y_i+Y_j,
\]
and take $M=2T_0+2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:separation}, with $B=4$, implies that
distinct two-term label vectors of $\ell^1$ norm at most $2$ differ in their
label contribution by at least $M$, and the unlabelled offsets can change a
distance by at most $2T_0$. For the three sign patterns above, a nonzero
two-term label vector determines the relevant packet type and its indices, up
to the harmless order of the two summands in $X_i+X_j$ or $Y_i+Y_j$.

Define
\[
 A=\bigcup_{i=1}^q(\ell_i+X_i)
 \ \cup\
 \bigcup_{i=1}^q(c_i-g_i-\ell_i+Y_i).
\]
The matched sums $(\ell_i+X_i)+(c_i-g_i-\ell_i+Y_i)$ are precisely the
zero-label packets considered above. These packets lie in
$[c_i-g_i,c_i+n+g_i]$. Since $g_i<n$ and $c_{i+1}-c_i\geq3n$, distinct
zero-label packets are separated by at least one missing integer.

Every unmatched packet has no adjacent elements. Indeed, $X_i+X_j$ lies in
one residue class modulo $2$, $Y_i+Y_j$ lies in one residue class modulo $3$,
and, for $i\neq j$, $X_i+Y_j$ lies in one residue class modulo $p_j$.
Because different nonzero label packets are separated from one another and
from the zero-label packets, no interval of length $n$ can be formed by an
unmatched packet or by joining different packets. Thus the only intervals
$[x,x+n]\subseteq2A$ are the prescribed intervals $[c_i,c_i+n]$.

Finally,
\[
 |A|\leq\sum_{i=1}^q\bigl((R_i+1)+(S_i+1)\bigr).
\]
Since $a_i\geq\sqrt n/2$ and $a_i\leq\sqrt n$, the estimates used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-single} give
\[
 R_i+1\leq 3\sqrt n+1,\qquad S_i+1\leq2\sqrt n.
\]
Summing over $i$ proves the cardinality bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The pair-sum separation hypothesis in Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-pair-separated}
is exactly what prevents the same-colour sums $Y_i+Y_j$ from interlacing. Thus
that theorem has a smaller construction and a milder large-$n$ threshold when
the additional additive separation is available. The CRT construction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-crt} removes that additive hypothesis by assigning
incompatible colours to different starts, but it asks for larger spacing
between the prescribed intervals and has constants depending on $q$. Neither
sparse argument replaces Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for complete isolation and
arbitrary placement of the starts.
\end{remark}

\section{A polynomial diameter refinement}

The power labels in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} make the first construction
transparent, but their size is exponential in the number of target points.
The following finite moment encoding gives a polynomial bound for fixed $h$.

\begin{lemma}[Moment separation]\label{lem:moment-separation}
Let $m,L\geq 1$, put
\[
 R=\max(1,m-1),
 \qquad
 K=LR^{L-1},
 \qquad
 Q=LK=L^2R^{L-1},
\]
and define
\[
 b_r=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^jQ^j
 \qquad (0\leq r<m).
\]
If $z=(z_0,\ldots,z_{m-1})\in\Z^m$ is nonzero and
$\lVert z\rVert_1\leq L$, then
\[
 \left|\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}z_rb_r\right|\geq 1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $0\leq j<L$, put
\[
 \mu_j=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}z_rr^j.
\]
Not all $\mu_j$ vanish. Indeed, if the support of $z$ has size $s$, then
$s\leq L$, and the first $s$ equations $\mu_j=0$ would form an invertible
Vandermonde system on the distinct support indices.

Since $|\mu_j|\leq LR^{L-1}=K$, the moment vector has
$\ell^1$ norm at most $LK=Q$. A nonzero integer coefficient vector with
$\ell^1$ norm at most $Q$ cannot evaluate to zero in base $Q$: if $J$ is
its largest nonzero index, the leading term has absolute value at least
$Q^J$, while all lower terms together have absolute value at most
$(Q-1)Q^{J-1}$ when $J\geq 1$; the case $J=0$ is immediate. Applying this
positional domination to $(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_{L-1})$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial diameter realization]\label{thm:polynomial}
Let $h\geq 2$, $d\geq 2$, and let $S\subseteq\Z$ be a nonempty finite set
with $m=|S|$. There is an explicitly constructible set $A\subseteq\Z$ with
$|A|=2m$ such that
\[
 hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
\]
where
\[
 |x-y|\geq d
\]
whenever $x,y\in E$ are distinct or $x\in E$ and $y\in S$.

More precisely, let $\tau\in h\Z$ minimize
\[
 T=\max_{t\in S}|t-\tau|.
\]
Put
\[
 L=2h(h-1),
 \quad
 R=\max(1,m-1),
 \quad
 Q=L^2R^{L-1},
\]
and
\[
 P_h(m)=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(m-1)^jQ^j.
\]
Then $A$ can be chosen with
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)
 \leq h(2hT+d)P_h(m)+T.
\]
For fixed $h$, the factor $P_h(m)$ is bounded by a polynomial in $m$ of
degree $L(L-1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Enumerate $S=\{t_0,\ldots,t_{m-1}\}$ and use the parameters in
Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-separation}. Set
\[
 M=2hT+d,
 \qquad
 a_r=M\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^jQ^j,
\]
and first construct the centered set
\[
 A_0=
 \{t_r-\tau+(h-1)a_r:0\leq r<m\}
 \cup
 \{-a_r:0\leq r<m\}.
\]
Finally, put
\[
 A=A_0+\tau/h.
\]

If $\lambda\neq\mu$ and
$\lVert\lambda\rVert_1+\lVert\mu\rVert_1\leq L$, then
$z=\lambda-\mu$ is nonzero and $\lVert z\rVert_1\leq L$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:moment-separation}, after multiplication by $M$, therefore
verifies \eqref{eq:abstract-separation} for the centered target set.
Proposition~\ref{prop:labels} gives $|A_0|=2m$, the matching-label
decomposition, and the required distance bounds.
Translating $A_0$ by $\tau/h$ translates $hA_0$ by $\tau$ and does not change
its diameter.

The labels $a_r$ are strictly increasing. Every element of $A_0$ lies between
$-a_{m-1}$ and $T+(h-1)a_{m-1}$. Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)=\operatorname{diam}(A_0)
 \leq ha_{m-1}+T
 =h(2hT+d)P_h(m)+T.
\]
For $m\geq 2$, the displayed expression for $P_h(m)$ is a polynomial of
degree $L(L-1)$; the case $m=1$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Translation-invariant interval bound]\label{cor:poly-interval}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, let
\[
 m=q(n+1),
 \qquad
 D=c_q+n-c_1.
\]
There is a set $A$ with the same isolated interval pattern and
$|A|=2q(n+1)$ whose diameter is polynomial in $m$ for fixed $h$, with
\[
 T\leq \left\lceil\frac{D+h}{2}\right\rceil
\]
in the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial}. In particular, the bound is
independent of the absolute location of the prescribed intervals.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial} to
$S=\bigcup_i[c_i,c_i+n]$. A multiple $\tau$ of $h$ can be chosen within
$h/2$ of the midpoint of $[c_1,c_q+n]$, giving the stated bound for $T$.
\end{proof}

\section{A Bose--Chowla diameter refinement}

The finite moment labels are elementary and formalized, but their polynomial
degree is not competitive. A classical construction of Bose and Chowla gives
small modular $B_t$ sets, and these can be converted into integer labels with
no short relations. We record the conversion explicitly because it is the
part used by the present construction.

Let $G$ be an abelian group. A set $B\subseteq G$ is a $B_t$ set if equality
of two $t$-term sums from $B$, with repetitions allowed, forces equality of
the two multisets of summands.

\begin{lemma}[Modular $B_t$ labels]\label{lem:modular-bt-labels}
Let $t\geq 1$, let $N\geq 1$, and let
$\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{m-1}\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ have distinct residue
classes forming a $B_t$ set in $\Z/N\Z$. Put
\[
 \gamma_r=(2t+1)N+\beta_r.
\]
If $z=(z_0,\ldots,z_{m-1})\in\Z^m$ is nonzero and
$\lVert z\rVert_1\leq 2t$, then
\[
 \sum_{r=0}^{m-1}z_r\gamma_r\neq 0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $z=z^+-z^-$, where $z_r^+=\max(z_r,0)$ and
$z_r^-=\max(-z_r,0)$. Let
\[
 r_+=\sum_r z_r^+,
 \qquad
 r_-=\sum_r z_r^-.
\]
Then $r_++r_-\leq 2t$. Suppose, for a contradiction, that
$\sum_rz_r\gamma_r=0$.

If $r_+\neq r_-$, then the contribution of the common shift $(2t+1)N$ has
absolute value at least $(2t+1)N$. The residue contribution has absolute
value at most
\[
 \sum_r(z_r^++z_r^-)(N-1)<2tN,
\]
which is too small to cancel the shift contribution. Hence $r_+=r_-=r$.
In particular, $r\leq t$.

Reducing the equality modulo $N$ gives equality in $\Z/N\Z$ between two
$r$-term sums of the residues $\beta_i$. Choose any one residue $\beta_0$
and pad both sides by $t-r$ copies of it. The $B_t$ property implies that
the two original $r$-term multisets are equal, so $z=0$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bose--Chowla diameter realization]\label{thm:bose-chowla}
Let $h\geq 2$, $d\geq 2$, and let $S\subseteq\Z$ be a nonempty finite set
with $m=|S|$. Put
\[
 H=h(h-1).
\]
For every prime power $Q\geq m$, there is an explicitly constructible set
$A\subseteq\Z$ with $|A|=2m$ such that
\[
 hA=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
\]
where distinct elements of $E$ are at distance at least $d$ from one another
and every element of $E$ is at distance at least $d$ from $S$. Moreover, if
$\tau\in h\Z$ minimizes
\[
 T=\max_{s\in S}|s-\tau|,
\]
then $A$ can be chosen with
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)
 \leq h(2hT+d)(2H+2)(Q^H-1)+T.
\]
Consequently, for $m\geq 2$ one may take a prime $Q\leq 2m-1$, giving
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)
 \leq h(2hT+d)(2H+2)\bigl((2m-1)^H-1\bigr)+T.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Bose--Chowla theorem~\cite{BoseChowla}, there is a $B_H$ set of
cardinality $Q$ in the cyclic group $\Z/(Q^H-1)\Z$. Choose any $m$ of its
elements and represent them by integers
$\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{m-1}\in[0,Q^H-2]$. Lemma~\ref{lem:modular-bt-labels}
with $t=H$ shows that
\[
 \gamma_r=(2H+1)(Q^H-1)+\beta_r
\]
has no nonzero integer relation of $\ell^1$ norm at most $2H$.

Enumerate the centered target set $S-\tau$ as $t_0,\ldots,t_{m-1}$ and put
\[
 M=2hT+d,
 \qquad
 a_r=M\gamma_r.
\]
The labels $a_r$ satisfy the separation hypothesis
\eqref{eq:abstract-separation}. Proposition~\ref{prop:labels} realizes the
centered target set. Translating the resulting basis by $\tau/h$ restores
$S$ and does not change the diameter.

Finally,
\[
 \max_r\gamma_r
 \leq (2H+1)(Q^H-1)+(Q^H-2)
 < (2H+2)(Q^H-1),
\]
and every centered generator lies between $-\max_r a_r$ and
$T+(h-1)\max_r a_r$. This gives the displayed diameter bound. The final
estimate follows from Bertrand's postulate, choosing a prime
$Q\leq 2m-1$ for $m\geq 2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic diameter for double sumsets]\label{thm:sidon}
Let $d\geq 2$, and let $S\subseteq\Z$ be a nonempty finite set with $m=|S|$.
There is an explicitly constructible set $A\subseteq\Z$ with $|A|=2m$ such
that
\[
 2A=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}E,
\]
where distinct elements of $E$ are at distance at least $d$ from one another
and every element of $E$ is at distance at least $d$ from $S$.

More precisely, let $\tau\in 2\Z$ minimize
\[
 T=\max_{t\in S}|t-\tau|,
\]
and let $p\geq m$ be an odd prime. For $0\leq r<m$, let $\rho_r$ be the least
nonnegative residue of $r^2$ modulo $p$, and put
\[
 b_r=2p^2+2pr+\rho_r.
\]
Then $A$ can be chosen with
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)\leq 8(4T+d)p^2+T.
\]
For $m\geq 2$, Bertrand's postulate allows $p<2m$, giving a quadratic bound
in $m$. Since $p$ is an integer, the resulting explicit estimate is
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)
 \leq 8(4T+d)(2m-1)^2+T.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First observe that $\{b_0,\ldots,b_{m-1}\}$ is a Sidon set, with repetitions
allowed in pair sums. Indeed, suppose
\[
 b_i+b_j=b_k+b_\ell.
\]
Reducing modulo $2p$ gives $\rho_i+\rho_j=\rho_k+\rho_\ell$, because both sides
lie between $0$ and $2p-2$. It follows that $i+j=k+\ell$. Reducing the residue
identity modulo $p$ also gives
\[
 i^2+j^2\equiv k^2+\ell^2\pmod p.
\]
Since $p$ is odd, we obtain $ij\equiv k\ell\pmod p$. Thus the two pairs are the
roots of the same quadratic polynomial over $\mathbb F_p$. The unordered
pairs $\{i,j\}$ and $\{k,\ell\}$ agree modulo $p$, hence agree as integer pairs
because all indices lie in $[0,p)$.

Moreover,
\[
 2p^2\leq b_r<4p^2.
\]
Consequently, an equality between two sums of the $b_r$ involving at most four
terms in total must have the same number of terms on both sides: one term is
strictly smaller than any sum of two terms. Equal one-term sums are trivial,
and equal two-term sums are trivial by the Sidon property. Therefore
\[
 \sum_r z_rb_r\neq 0
\]
for every nonzero integer vector $z$ with $\lVert z\rVert_1\leq 4$.

Set $M=4T+d$, enumerate $S=\{t_0,\ldots,t_{m-1}\}$, and apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:labels} to the centered targets $t_r-\tau$ and the labels
$a_r=Mb_r$. Finally translate the resulting basis by $\tau/2$. Since
$b_r<4p^2$, its diameter is at most
\[
 2M\max_r b_r+T\leq 8(4T+d)p^2+T.
\]
The same construction has quadratic diameter from below. Indeed, translation
does not change diameter, the negative generators contain
$-Mb_0$ and $-Mb_{m-1}$, and
\[
 b_{m-1}-b_0
 =2p(m-1)+\rho_{m-1}
 \geq 2m(m-1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(A)\geq 2M m(m-1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A Sidon-label diameter barrier]\label{prop:sidon-barrier}
Let $B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_m\}\subseteq\Z$ be a Sidon set, where repetitions are
allowed in pair sums. Then
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(B)\geq \binom{m}{2}.
\]
Consequently, any tagged construction that contains the generators
$-Mb_i$ has diameter at least $M\binom{m}{2}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Order the labels so that $b_1<\cdots<b_m$. The $\binom{m}{2}$ positive
differences $b_j-b_i$, with $i<j$, are distinct. Indeed, an equality
$b_j-b_i=b_\ell-b_k$ gives $b_j+b_k=b_\ell+b_i$, and the Sidon property
forces the two ordered index pairs to agree. Every positive difference is at
most $\operatorname{diam}(B)$, proving the first claim. The second follows
because the generators $-Mb_i$ already have diameter
$M\operatorname{diam}(B)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Elementary lower bounds]\label{rem:lower-bounds}
Let $A\subseteq\Z$ be finite and nonempty, put $k=|A|$, and suppose that
$S\subseteq hA$ has $m$ elements. Then
\[
 m\leq \binom{k+h-1}{h},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{diam}(A)\geq \frac{\operatorname{diam}(S)}{h}.
\]
Indeed, the first inequality counts unordered $h$-term multisets from $A$,
and the second follows from
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(hA)=h\operatorname{diam}(A).
\]
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial} supplies an elementary polynomial diameter
bound for fixed $h$, while Theorem~\ref{thm:bose-chowla} lowers the exponent
to $H=h(h-1)$ by using classical modular $B_H$ sets. Theorem~\ref{thm:sidon}
gives a quadratic bound when $h=2$ with better constants and a direct
integer formula. Proposition~\ref{prop:sidon-barrier} shows that the
quadratic order is unavoidable within the Sidon-label method. Optimizing the
cardinality for the arbitrary multi-interval isolated problem, or proving
stronger lower bounds outside these label frameworks, remains open.
\end{remark}

\section{Relation with the single-interval construction}

Nathanson's version 3 contains a direct construction for one prescribed
interval in a double sumset. The special case $q=1$ and $h=2$ of
Corollary~\ref{cor:single} gives another route to the same type of
single-interval conclusion, but this is not the main point of the present
paper. The label construction is designed to make independent cancellations
for many target points coexist without producing additional adjacent
exceptional sums. This is what allows the same argument to treat the
multi-interval questions and the higher-fold case uniformly.

The quantitative refinements also belong to the finite-target viewpoint.
Moment labels make the construction polynomial in the number of target
points for fixed $h$, after centering the target set. For $h=2$, shifted
Sidon labels give a quadratic diameter bound, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:sidon-barrier} shows that this order is forced by the
Sidon-label mechanism itself.

The sparse construction in Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-single} points in a
different direction: it optimizes cardinality rather than isolation. It shows
that the $2n+2$ size in the isolated single-interval specialization is not a
lower bound for the starts-only single-interval problem.
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse-lower} shows that the resulting $O(\sqrt n)$
cardinality is optimal up to the absolute constant.
Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-pair-separated} extends the same packet to several
starts when the pair-sum set $C+C$ is separated. The incompatible-colour
construction in Theorem~\ref{thm:sparse-crt} removes the pair-sum condition
for fixed $q$ and large $n$, at the cost of a larger spacing hypothesis.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} remains the arbitrary-placement and complete-isolation
result.

\section{Reproducibility}

The accompanying Python package constructs $A$, computes $hA$, checks all
maximal consecutive runs, records the minimum exceptional spacing, and emits
JSON certificates. It also includes exact small-parameter computations
cross-checked against \href{https://oeis.org/A001208}{OEIS A001208} and
\href{https://oeis.org/A001209}{OEIS A001209}, together with bounded scans for
sets whose double sumset has exactly one nontrivial interval. These bounded
scans are used only as discovery evidence for cardinality questions. The
arithmetic separation,
bounded-offset, matched-count, tagged-representation, exceptional-count
uniqueness, generic set-level isolation, interval-enumeration, maximal-run,
and exact-cardinality arguments have also been checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib.
The resulting Lean theorem automatically selects the target radius and
substitutes the displayed values of $M$ and $B$. The finite moment-label
Vandermonde argument, translation rule, closed diameter estimate, exact
cardinality, midpoint-centering lemma, and interval-family theorem have also
been formalized. For the shifted-Sidon refinement, Lean checks the size
bounds and the reduction from
order-four short-relation freedom to set-level isolation, including the
explicit pair-sum argument over $\mathbb F_p$. It also checks translation
invariance, quadratic diameter upper and lower bounds, tightened
Bertrand-prime selection, exact cardinality after translation, and the final
separated-interval wrapper. The midpoint translation, radius, and scale are
selected automatically from an enclosing interval. The sparse Frobenius,
pair-sum-separated, and CRT-incompatible constructions are implemented in the
Python package and are included as ordinary paper proofs, not as Lean
theorems.
